How the break-even calculator works

The formula

The break-even point is when the EV's lower running costs have repaid its higher purchase price: break-even = (EV price − gas car price − incentives) ÷ (yearly gas car running cost − yearly EV running cost). Running costs come from the mileage, fuel, electricity and maintenance figures you enter.

Assumptions and limits

The model keeps prices and efficiency constant over time and does not include depreciation, insurance or financing unless you build them into your inputs. Use your own electricity rate (check your utility bill) and the local gasoline price. For a full comparison use the 5-year cost calculator.

Tips for better results

If you will use public fast chargers often, enter a higher electricity price. Federal EV credits ended for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025, so enter only state or utility rebates you have verified. See the true cost of owning an EV.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good break-even time?

It should be shorter than the time you plan to keep the car. The answer depends heavily on mileage, energy prices and where you charge.

Why is my result "never"?

If the EV's yearly running cost is not lower than the gas car's, the price gap is never repaid by fuel savings alone.
