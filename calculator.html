Methodology

How the numbers are calculated

01
Monthly Gas Cost

(Daily miles × 30.5 days) ÷ MPG × gas price. This is what you're spending on fuel right now.

02
Monthly Charging Cost

(Daily miles × 30.5) ÷ mi/kWh × your state's electricity rate. Assumes primarily home charging.

03
CO₂ Calculation

US average of 19.6 lbs CO₂ per gallon of gas burned. EV grid emissions vary; we use the US average grid factor (0.92 lbs/kWh) for EV offset.

Disclaimer: Calculations are estimates for informational purposes. Actual savings depend on your specific driving patterns, utility rate schedule, EV charging behavior, and vehicle condition. Electricity rates sourced from US EIA data (2025–2026 averages).

How the EV savings calculator works

The calculation

Yearly savings = gas car running cost − EV running cost. Gas car cost is miles driven ÷ miles per gallon × gas price, plus maintenance. EV cost is miles driven ÷ miles per kWh × electricity price, plus maintenance.

Where to get realistic inputs

Look up a car's fuel economy and efficiency on fueleconomy.gov, check average energy prices from the U.S. Energy Information Administration (eia.gov), and use the rate on your own electricity bill for the most accurate result.

Limits

The calculator does not include purchase price, depreciation, insurance or the cost of a home charger. Combine it with our 5-year TCO calculator and our guide to home charging.

Frequently asked questions

How much can I save with an EV?

It depends on your mileage, local fuel and electricity prices, and where you charge. Home charging usually saves the most.

Are public chargers more expensive?

Often, yes. DC fast charging can cost several times more per kWh than home charging, which can reduce or remove the savings.
